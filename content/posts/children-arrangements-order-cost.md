Parents often sort out where a child lives, and how the child spends time with each of them, by agreeing it between themselves. If you want the family court in England or Wales to make a child arrangements order, the fee to start that application is **£270**. The fee has applied since 13 July 2026. It pays the court to open the case. A mediator's fee and a solicitor's fee are separate. The official fees in this guide were checked on 21 November 2026.

People still search for this under the older word "custody". The order itself sets out the practical arrangements: who the child lives with, and who the child spends time with. The court puts the child's welfare first, and it can decide that no order is needed. You can [compare family law fees](/family-law/). The price firms list for children arrangements on Compare Legal is for a first consultation, which is the right place to start. A longer case is usually charged by the hour after that.

Money and property between the adults are a different application. [Consent order costs](/blog/consent-order-cost/) covers that, and [divorce costs](/blog/divorce-costs-uk/) covers ending the marriage.

## England and Wales

The fees are in the family court fees guide, EX50, updated on 26 October 2026. Wales uses the same fees. Cafcass acts in England, and Cafcass Cymru in Wales. Either may be asked to speak with the family and tell the court what it thinks will help the child.

| Application | Court fee from 13 July 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| A new application for a child arrangements order, a specific issue order or a prohibited steps order | £270 |
| An enforcement order | £270 |
| An application following the breach of an enforcement order | £119 |
| An application by consent, or without notice, in a case the court is already hearing | £62 |
| An application on notice in a case the court is already hearing | £195 |

The £270 fee is for a new application, on form C100, when the court is not already dealing with proceedings about that child. If a case is already open, the smaller fees in the last two rows are the ones to check. People on a low income or certain benefits can apply for [Help with Fees](https://www.gov.uk/get-help-with-court-fees), which can cover the court fee.

Before most applications you go to a Mediation Information and Assessment Meeting, so a mediator can explain whether mediation might help you reach an agreement. The mediator sets the charge for that meeting. GOV.UK lists situations where a meeting is not required, including some cases where there is evidence of domestic abuse, and applications that are urgent. If you or a child are at risk of harm, tell a solicitor or the court at the start. They can explain the protective applications that exist alongside arrangements for a child's time.

Legal aid for children cases is available only in limited situations. [GOV.UK explains who qualifies](https://www.gov.uk/legal-aid).

A solicitor represents one parent. The first meeting is often a fixed fee, and time after that is usually charged by the hour. You can apply yourself, including where you are asking the court to record terms you have both agreed. The court still decides whether an order is in the child's interests.

## Scotland

Scotland uses a residence order and a contact order, under section 11 of the Children (Scotland) Act 1995. The court treats the child's welfare as its paramount consideration.

Most of these applications start in the sheriff court. The fee for the initial writ is **£176** from 1 April 2026, under the Sheriff Court Fees Order 2026. If the other person opposes the application, the fee for them to state a defence is £160. Later steps, such as a written motion, have further fees. The Scottish Courts and Tribunals Service publishes the full table.

You may pay none of those sheriff court fees if you are receiving civil legal aid for the case, or if you receive certain benefits, including Universal Credit. The Scottish Legal Aid Board explains advice and assistance, which can help with a solicitor's initial work, and full civil legal aid. You can [compare family law fees in Scotland](/family-law/scotland/).

Where arrangements for a child are already part of a sheriff court divorce, they are included in that action. The fee to start an ordinary divorce is £191 from 1 April 2026. [Divorce costs](/blog/divorce-costs-uk/) explains the rest of that process.

## Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland uses contact orders and residence orders, under Article 8 of the Children (Northern Ireland) Order 1995, together with prohibited steps orders and specific issue orders. The child's welfare is the court's paramount consideration.

The fee depends on which court you apply to. The figures below apply from 1 April 2026.

| Court | Fee to apply for an Article 8 order |
| --- | --- |
| Family Proceedings Court (magistrates' court) | £81 |
| County court | £123 |
| High Court | £163 |

In the magistrates' court, an application to vary, extend or discharge that order is £60. Serving the summons is a further fee: £17, or £22 where personal service is required, under the Magistrates' Courts Fees (Amendment) Order (Northern Ireland) 2026. The county court and High Court figures are in the Family Proceedings Fees (Amendment) Order (Northern Ireland) 2026.

An application for a non-molestation order or an occupation order, under the Family Homes and Domestic Violence (Northern Ireland) Order 1998, has no court fee in the magistrates' court, the county court or the High Court.

The Department of Justice explains fee exemption and remission for people on a low income or certain benefits. You can [compare family law fees in Northern Ireland](/family-law/northern-ireland/).

## What changes the solicitor's bill

The court fee is a set amount. The solicitor's bill follows the amount of work.

- An agreement you can both keep to may need no court case at all
- Asking the court to record an agreement is usually a smaller piece of work than a dispute
- Several hearings, long statements, or a need for an expert report all add time
- Enforcing or changing an order later is a new application, with its own fee

Before you instruct someone, it helps to ask:

1. Is the first meeting a fixed fee, and what does it cover?
2. If the case continues, what is the hourly rate, and who does the work?
3. Does any estimate include VAT, the court fee, and a barrister if one is needed?
4. What has to happen before an application, including mediation?
5. How will urgent concerns about a child be dealt with?

[Questions to ask a solicitor](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-instructing-a-solicitor/) goes through the same points for any area of work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is £270 the total cost of a child arrangements order?

It is the court fee to start a new application in England and Wales. A mediator and a solicitor charge separately. Help with Fees may cover the court fee if you are on a low income or certain benefits. A case that is agreed, and a case with several hearings, are very different amounts of a solicitor's time.

### Do both parents need a solicitor?

Each parent can take their own advice, and a solicitor acts for one of them. You can also apply yourself. If you have agreed the arrangements, you can ask the court to make an order in those terms. The court still decides whether an order is the right outcome for the child.

### What if we agree already?

You may not need an order. Many parents write down what they have agreed and leave it there. If you want the court to make it an order, you still pay the application fee. In England and Wales you usually attend a mediation information meeting first, unless an exemption applies. The solicitor's work is usually shorter than in a case where the arrangements are disputed.

### Does this fee include child maintenance?

Child maintenance is separate. The Child Maintenance Service deals with how much maintenance is paid. A child arrangements order is about where a child lives and the time they spend with each parent.
