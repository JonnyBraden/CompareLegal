Confirmation is the document a Scottish sheriff court issues to the executors of someone who has died. Banks, insurers and the property registers ask for it before they will release assets that were in the person's sole name. From 1 April 2026 the court fee is based on the value of the estate for which confirmation is required. It is nothing up to £50,000, **£351** from £50,000.01 to £250,000, and **£705** above £250,000. A solicitor's fee is separate. The figures in this guide were checked on 21 November 2026.

You can [compare confirmation fees](/probate/scotland/) for a confirmation-only service or full executry. [Probate costs across the UK](/blog/probate-costs-uk/) puts the English, Welsh and Northern Irish court fees next to these. [Fixed fee or percentage](/blog/probate-fixed-fee-vs-percentage/) is a way to compare the solicitor's charge, which is the larger and more variable part.

## The sheriff court fee

The bands are in the Sheriff Court Fees Order 2026. They use the value of the estate vested in or belonging beneficially to the person who died, of which confirmation is required: in short, the estate you list in the inventory as needing confirmation.

| Value of the estate for which confirmation is required | Court fee from 1 April 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Up to £50,000 | No fee |
| £50,000.01 to £250,000 | £351 |
| Over £250,000 | £705 |

A further table of fees applies from 1 April 2027, so check the Scottish Courts and Tribunals Service list if you apply after that date.

Extra documents are cheaper if you order them when you lodge the inventory.

| Document, ordered with the inventory | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Certificate of confirmation, for a particular asset | £10 |
| Copy or duplicate confirmation | £15 |
| Certified extract of the confirmation and the will, if there is one | £33 |
| Petition to appoint an executor (a dative petition) | £23 |

The same documents ordered later cost more, because a search fee is added. A certificate of confirmation has the same effect as the confirmation for the asset it names, and it lets you approach more than one organisation at once.

You cannot claim the usual sheriff court fee exemption against the confirmation fee. The fee order says the exemptions for civil legal aid and for certain benefits do not apply to commissary fees, which is the name for this part of the court's work. The small-estates guidance says the same in plain terms: you cannot apply for a fee exemption when applying for confirmation, and fees for certificates and copies remain payable.

An additional or corrective inventory is charged on the same bands. That is the form you use if something was missed, or a value has to be changed.

## Small estates: £36,000 or under

The Scottish Courts and Tribunals Service treats an estate as small when the total value of the person's money and property is **£36,000 or less**. Work that figure out without deducting debts. The funeral, household bills and the balance of a mortgage stay in the total. Bank balances include interest up to the date of death.

For a small estate, the sheriff clerk can help you complete the inventory. Phone the sheriff clerk for the area where the person lived and book an appointment. If that court is awkward to get to, your local sheriff clerk can complete the inventory instead. Take the small-estate checklist. For a death on or after 1 January 2022 the form the clerk completes with you is form C1. Court staff cannot give legal advice, and they cannot help you divide the estate once confirmation has been issued.

Because a small estate is under £50,000, the court fee for the confirmation itself is nil. You still pay for any certificates or copies you order.

A large estate, meaning anything over £36,000, is one the court service will not help you prepare. It points people to a solicitor. The Law Society of Scotland can give you names.

## If there is no will

Where there is no will, the person who wants to be executor may need a **bond of caution**. It is insurance against someone taking up the estate who is not entitled to, and against an executor failing to distribute it as the law requires. The premium is set by the insurer. The court does not publish a premium.

Two points from the Scottish Courts and Tribunals Service matter to the cost:

- If the estate is under £36,000 and the sheriff clerk helps prepare the inventory, a bond of caution is not required.
- If a solicitor prepares the inventory in a case where a bond is required, you need the bond before confirmation is issued. The solicitor explains the steps and the premium.

If the estate is over £36,000 and there is no will, you also apply to be appointed executor, by a dative petition. The court fee for that petition is £23. The court service recommends legal advice for that application.

## What a solicitor charges

Solicitors offer confirmation only, or the full executry. Confirmation only is often a fixed fee: the firm prepares the inventory and the tax forms, and you collect the assets. Full executry is the collecting, the sale or transfer of property, the debts, the tax and the payments to beneficiaries. Firms charge a fixed fee, an hourly rate, or a percentage of the estate. Ask for any percentage as a pound figure for this estate before you compare it with a fixed fee. VAT is added to the legal fee.

## Inheritance tax, and when you may not need confirmation

Inheritance tax uses the same rules across the UK. Most estates pay none. Where tax is due, some of it is usually paid before confirmation. The current bands are on the [inheritance tax](https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax) pages on GOV.UK.

You may not need confirmation if the organisation holding the money will release it without one. Each bank and insurer sets its own limit, so ask each of them. A house or flat in the sole name of the person who died almost always needs confirmation before it can be transferred or sold. Selling it is a separate instruction: [compare conveyancing fees in Scotland](/conveyancing/scotland/).

A spouse or civil partner, and children, may have legal rights in the moveable estate whatever a will says. That can add work, and it is one reason a Scottish will is drafted with those rights in mind. [Wills across the UK](/blog/wills-and-powers-of-attorney-uk-differences/) explains legal rights.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does confirmation cost in 2026?

From 1 April 2026 the sheriff court fee is nil up to £50,000, £351 up to £250,000, and £705 above that. Certificates and copies are extra if you need them. A solicitor's fee for preparing the application, or for the full executry, is on top.

### Is confirmation the same as probate?

It does the same job. Executors apply to the sheriff court with an inventory of the estate, and the court issues confirmation. The fee depends on the value of that estate. England and Wales charge a flat £526 probate fee above £5,000. [Probate costs across the UK](/blog/probate-costs-uk/) compares the three systems.

### Can I apply for confirmation myself?

Yes. If the estate is £36,000 or less, the sheriff clerk will help you prepare the inventory, by appointment. Above that, you prepare it yourself or with a solicitor. A solicitor is worth considering when there is a house, a business, inheritance tax to pay, or no will.

### Do I pay a court fee for a small estate?

The confirmation itself has no court fee when the value for which you need confirmation is £50,000 or under, which includes every small estate. Copies and certificates still have a fee. You cannot apply to have the confirmation fee waived. If you use a solicitor, their fee is separate.
