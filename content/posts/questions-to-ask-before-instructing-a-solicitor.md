Questions to ask a solicitor before you instruct include what the total price covers, who will do the work, and how to complain if something goes wrong. To compare solicitor quotes, and to compare conveyancing quotes, use the total of the legal fee, VAT and disbursements.

They apply when you are [comparing conveyancing fees](/conveyancing/), [making a will](/wills/), [dealing with an estate](/probate/) or [arranging a divorce](/family-law/), in any UK nation. The firm must be qualified there. Estates use a grant of probate in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and confirmation in Scotland.

**Compare the total, not the legal fee.** Add the legal fee, VAT and disbursements, and on a purchase the property tax. Disbursements, often called outlays in Scotland and Northern Ireland, are what the firm pays to others for you. The tax is Stamp Duty Land Tax in England and Northern Ireland, Land Transaction Tax in Wales, and Land and Buildings Transaction Tax in Scotland, and it is the same for every firm. [How to read a conveyancing quote](/blog/conveyancing-quote-explained-england-wales/) and [conveyancing disbursements explained](/blog/conveyancing-disbursements-explained/) set out the lines.

## What should I ask about the price?

### 1. Is this a fixed fee, and what would make it go up?

Fixed fees are common for a straightforward purchase or sale, a simple will or an uncontested divorce. Ask what falls outside, such as a leasehold, a new build, extra lender requirements, an unregistered title, a dispute or overseas assets, and get the answer in writing.

### 2. Does the total include VAT and all disbursements?

Ask for VAT and for every disbursement by name, or every outlay in Scotland and Northern Ireland. On Compare Legal each total includes VAT and the disbursements the firm has listed.

### 3. Will I pay anything if the matter doesn't go ahead?

Ask whether the firm offers no completion, no fee, or what you would pay for work already done, and whether a search fee already paid to a third party is refundable.

### 4. Do you pay or receive any referral fees?

If an estate agent, broker or website suggested the firm, ask whether anyone is paid for that. You need not instruct that firm. In England and Wales a solicitor must tell you of any financial interest in a referral and any fee-sharing on your matter, and a licensed conveyancer must publish whether a referral fee is paid. Compare Legal is independent: firms list for free, and it takes no referral fee or commission for enquiries.

## What should I ask about who does the work?

### 5. Who will handle my case, and what are they qualified as?

Ask who will do the work, their qualification and who supervises them: a partner, a solicitor, a trainee or, in England and Wales, a licensed conveyancer or a chartered legal executive. In Scotland and Northern Ireland a solicitor does the conveyancing.

### 6. How will you keep me updated, and how quickly do you reply?

Ask how the firm will update you, and how quickly it aims to reply.

### 7. How many similar cases do you handle?

Ask whether the firm regularly does your sort of work, such as new builds, leasehold, Scottish purchases, confirmation, disputed estates, or arrangements for children.

### 8. How long is this likely to take?

Ask for a timescale and what usually causes delay. Firms often say around 8 to 12 weeks for a typical house purchase, and a year or more for a full estate administration.

## How do I check a solicitor is regulated?

### 9. Who regulates you, and are you insured?

In England and Wales, check the [Solicitors Register](https://www.sra.org.uk/consumers/register/). Solicitors are regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority, and emails should show the SRA number and that the firm is authorised and regulated by it. Licensed conveyancers are regulated by the Council for Licensed Conveyancers: use its [register](https://www.clc-uk.org/consumers/find-a-licensed-conveyancer/). Scotland and Northern Ireland have no licensed conveyancers. The [Law Society of Scotland](https://www.lawscot.org.uk/find-a-solicitor/) regulates Scottish solicitors, and the [Law Society of Northern Ireland](https://lawsoc-ni.org/using-a-solicitor/find-a-solicitor) regulates solicitors in Northern Ireland.

Solicitors in private practice, and licensed conveyancers, must hold professional indemnity insurance. In Scotland that cover is the Master Policy. England and Wales is a single jurisdiction. A firm qualified only in Scotland or only in Northern Ireland acts in that nation unless it is also qualified elsewhere.

### 10. How do I complain if something goes wrong?

Complain to the firm first, and check that nation's time limits on its official page.

In England and Wales the firm must tell you in writing, at the outset, how to complain about the service and the bill, and about the [Legal Ombudsman](https://www.legalombudsman.org.uk/for-consumers/factsheets/here-to-help). The Ombudsman handles service complaints about solicitors and licensed conveyancers, free of charge. Conduct goes to the Solicitors Regulation Authority, or the Council for Licensed Conveyancers.

In Scotland, after the firm's client relations manager, the [Scottish Legal Complaints Commission](https://www.scottishlegalcomplaints.org.uk/) is the gateway. It handles service and sends conduct complaints to the Law Society of Scotland.

In Northern Ireland, service complaints go to the [Solicitors Complaints Committee](https://www.scc-ni.org/) and conduct complaints to the Law Society of Northern Ireland. The [Legal Services Oversight Commissioner](https://www.legalcommissioner-ni.org.uk/role-commissioner) oversees that system and does not decide your complaint.

## How do I compare solicitor quotes side by side?

Use the same lines for every firm. [Conveyancing costs in England](/blog/conveyancing-costs-england/), [in Scotland](/blog/conveyancing-costs-scotland/) and [in Northern Ireland](/blog/conveyancing-costs-northern-ireland/) explain a purchase in each system.

| Line on the quote | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Legal fee | Whether it is fixed, and what falls outside it |
| VAT | Whether VAT is already included |
| Each disbursement | Named on its own |
| Property tax, on a purchase | Shown separately |
| Extras | Any leasehold, new-build or mortgage supplement, and whether it applies |
| If the matter stops | No completion, no fee, or what you pay for work already done |
| Who handles the matter | Name, qualification and supervisor |
| Regulator | Which body, and that the firm is on its register |

A lower legal fee with disbursements missing is not a lower total.

## What should I read before I sign?

Read the client care letter before you instruct. In England and Wales it covers the work, the likely cost, who is acting and how to complain, including the Legal Ombudsman. [Practice Rule B4](https://www.lawscot.org.uk/members/rules-and-guidance/rules-and-guidance/section-e/division-g/guidance/price-transparency/) in Scotland, and the [Solicitors (Client Communication) Practice Regulations 2008](https://lawsoc-ni.org/assets/general/files/regulations-and-standards/Solicitors-Client-Communication-Practice-Regulations-2008.pdf) in Northern Ireland, require the same in writing, with VAT and outlays.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it worth paying more for a local solicitor?

A local firm can meet you. Another firm in the same jurisdiction can work remotely, and must still be qualified there. Compare the total and who does the work.

### Can I change solicitor partway through?

Yes. You usually pay for work already done, and the new firm asks the old one for the file.

### Should I always choose the cheapest quote?

Compare totals for the same work. A lower legal fee can omit VAT or disbursements. Where the work, the person doing it and the fall-through terms match, the lower total is a fair reason to choose.

### What is a client care letter?

It is the engagement letter, also called terms of business. It sets out the work, the price, who will do it and how to complain. In every nation the firm has to give you this in writing.

### Do solicitors have to publish their prices?

In England and Wales, Solicitors Regulation Authority firms must publish prices for residential conveyancing and for uncontested work collecting and distributing assets in the UK after a death, plus some other listed services. Wills and divorce are not included. A firm with no website must still provide the information on request, and licensed conveyancers must publish conveyancing costs. In Scotland a written estimate is required when you instruct; publishing a price is guidance, not a rule. In Northern Ireland the written estimate is required at instruction, not a website price.
