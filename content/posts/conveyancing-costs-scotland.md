Conveyancing costs in Scotland are the solicitor's legal fee, VAT at 20% on that fee, and outlays, including Registers of Scotland registration dues. Buyers also pay Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) to Revenue Scotland, and that tax does not change with the firm you choose. You can [compare conveyancing fees in Scotland](/conveyancing/scotland/) with VAT and outlays in the total. Offers, the Home Report and missives are covered in [buying a house in Scotland](/blog/buying-a-house-in-scotland-conveyancing/).

## What should a Scottish conveyancing quote include?

The total is the legal fee, VAT on that fee, and outlays. England and Wales call outlays disbursements.

| Part of what you pay | Who sets it | Official source, checked 23 November 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal fee | The firm. No scale | [Law Society of Scotland](https://www.lawscot.org.uk/) |
| VAT | 20% of the legal fee | [VAT rates](https://www.gov.uk/vat-rates) (GOV.UK) |
| Disposition (the transfer) | Registers of Scotland, by price or value | [Registration fees](https://kb.ros.gov.uk/fees/registration-fees), updated 22 November 2026 |
| Standard security (the mortgage) | Registers of Scotland | £80 per title sheet |
| Advance notice | Registers of Scotland | £20 |
| Property enquiry certificate | The council | No Scotland-wide fee |
| LBTT | Revenue Scotland | [Residential rates](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/residential-property) |
| Additional Dwelling Supplement | Revenue Scotland, if it applies | 8% of the price from 5 December 2024 ([ADS](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/additional-dwelling-supplement-ads)) |
| Home Report | The seller, paid to a surveyor | [mygov.scot](https://www.mygov.scot/buy-home/home-report). No official fee |

[Conveyancing disbursements explained](/blog/conveyancing-disbursements-explained/) sets Scottish outlays beside the fees in England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

## What does Registers of Scotland charge?

The Land Register fee is the price in the deed or the value, whichever is greater. [Registers of Scotland](https://kb.ros.gov.uk/fees/registration-fees) updated its fees on 22 November 2026 (checked 23 November 2026), under the 2014 fees order, as amended in 2021, and the 2015 voluntary-registration order.

| Price or value | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| £0 to £50,000 | £80 |
| £50,001 to £100,000 | £140 |
| £100,001 to £150,000 | £260 |
| £150,001 to £200,000 | £400 |
| £200,001 to £300,000 | £530 |
| £300,001 to £500,000 | £660 |
| £500,001 to £700,000 | £800 |
| £700,001 to £1,000,000 | £930 |

Prices above £1,000,000 are on that page. £250,000 and £300,000 are **£530**; £300,001 is **£660**. An extra title sheet is **£80**, except some shared plots. A standard security is **£80** per title sheet, an advance notice is **£20**, and a discharge of the seller's security is **£80**, or **£60** digitally, usually on the seller's bill. A Sasine title uses this table when the disposition is registered in the Land Register.

## How much is Land and Buildings Transaction Tax?

Scotland charges LBTT. England and Northern Ireland charge Stamp Duty Land Tax, and Wales charges Land Transaction Tax. See [SDLT, LTT and LBTT compared](/blog/stamp-duty-sdlt-ltt-lbtt-compared/). Each rate applies only to its slice of the price. [Revenue Scotland](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/residential-property) shows these residential rates from 1 April 2021, still the published rates when checked 23 November 2026. The [Scottish Budget 2026 to 2027](https://www.gov.scot/publications/scottish-budget-2026-2027/pages/4/) (6 March 2026) keeps them for 2026-27.

| Part of the price | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to £145,000 | 0% |
| £145,001 to £250,000 | 2% |
| £250,001 to £325,000 | 5% |
| £325,001 to £750,000 | 10% |
| Over £750,000 | 12% |

Revenue Scotland's example of £235,000 is nothing on £145,000 and 2% on £90,000, so **£1,800**. The same bands make £300,000 **£4,600**: nothing on £145,000, **£2,100** at 2% and **£2,500** at 5%. The [calculator](https://revenue.scot/calculate-tax/calculate-property-transactions) covers any other price. Purchases of £40,000 or more need a return even when no tax is due. File and pay within 30 days of the day after the [effective date](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/lbtt-legislation-guidance/lbtt1001-how-tax-works/lbtt1004-effective-date), which on a standard purchase is the date of entry. Your solicitor usually files.

### What do first-time buyers pay?

The nil-rate band rises from £145,000 to £175,000, saving up to **£600** ([Revenue Scotland](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/lbtt-legislation-guidance/lbtt3001-exemptions-reliefs/lbtt3010-tax-reliefs/lbtt3048-first-time-buyer-relief)). At £300,000 the tax is **£4,000** (£4,600 less the £600 relief). Every buyer must never have owned a dwelling anywhere and must intend to live in the home as their only or main residence. One previous owner, or the Additional Dwelling Supplement, blocks the relief. Claim it on the return. The 2026-27 Budget keeps the £175,000 band.

### When does the Additional Dwelling Supplement apply?

From 5 December 2024 it is **8% of the price**, on top of LBTT, for an extra home that does not replace your only or main residence. The 2026-27 Budget keeps 8%. At £300,000 that is **£24,000**, so **£28,600** with LBTT of £4,600.

Under £40,000 there is no supplement. From 1 April 2024, selling your only or main residence in the previous 36 months can keep the charge off. If you still own that home, selling it within the next 36 months can support a repayment, once you have lived in the new home. Companies and many trusts pay on a first dwelling. Joint buyers are on the [ADS page](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/additional-dwelling-supplement-ads), checked 23 November 2026.

## Who pays for the Home Report?

The seller gets the Home Report, and pays the surveyor, before marketing. [mygov.scot](https://www.mygov.scot/sell-home/first-steps) describes the three parts: a single survey and valuation, a property questionnaire and an energy report. There is no official survey fee. The seller must provide the Home Report within nine days of a request ([Home Report](https://www.mygov.scot/buy-home/home-report)). Trading standards can fine the seller **£500** if they do not. New houses sold off-plan or to the first occupier are exempt, but an Energy Performance Certificate is still required.

## Is the solicitor's fee fixed or hourly?

There is no scale for the legal fee. A firm may quote a fixed fee or an hourly rate. Ask whether the fee includes noting interest, offers, the lender and the LBTT return, and what you pay if missives are never concluded.

[Best-reviewed conveyancing solicitors in Scotland](/blog/best-conveyancing-solicitors-scotland/) summarises client comments on firms in Glasgow, Edinburgh, Aberdeen and Dundee, and says no firm paid to be included. The solicitor must be qualified in Scots law. A firm qualified only in England and Wales, or only in Northern Ireland, cannot act.

## How do missives affect what you pay?

Missives are the letters that form the contract. Once concluded, both sides are bound, which can be weeks before the date of entry. England and Wales usually exchange later. Agree the mortgage offer, and read the Home Report, before you conclude: withdrawing afterwards can leave you liable for the seller's losses. Offers and closing dates are in [buying a house in Scotland](/blog/buying-a-house-in-scotland-conveyancing/).

## How should you compare conveyancing quotes in Scotland?

1. **Legal fee**, fixed or hourly.
2. **VAT**, and whether 20% is already in the figure.
3. **Outlays**: disposition, standard security, any advance notice, and the property enquiry certificate.
4. **LBTT** and any Additional Dwelling Supplement, separate from the fee.
5. **Scope**: lender, LBTT return, noting interest, further offers.
6. **A file that stops** before missives are concluded.
7. **A solicitor** qualified in Scots law and regulated by the Law Society of Scotland.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does conveyancing cost in Scotland?

Add the firm's legal fee, VAT at 20% and outlays. Registration of a disposition at £200,001 to £300,000 is £530. LBTT on a £300,000 home is £4,600 for someone who is not a first-time buyer.

### What are outlays on a Scottish purchase?

Outlays are payments your solicitor makes for you, including Registers of Scotland dues and a council property enquiry certificate. England and Wales call the same items disbursements. Each council sets its own certificate fee.

### How much is LBTT on a £300,000 home?

£4,600 from the bands above. A qualifying first-time buyer pays £4,000. Where the Additional Dwelling Supplement applies, add £24,000.

### Do first-time buyers pay LBTT in Scotland?

The nil-rate band rises from £145,000 to £175,000, a saving of up to £600. Every buyer must qualify, and the Additional Dwelling Supplement blocks the relief. At £40,000 or more you still file a return when no tax is due.

### Who pays for the Home Report?

The seller pays the surveyor. You receive the survey, the questionnaire and the energy report. There is no official fee for that survey, or for one you commission yourself.

### Can a solicitor from England act on a house in Scotland?

Only a solicitor qualified in Scots law can act. The Law Society of Scotland regulates the profession. An England and Wales firm, or a Northern Ireland firm, cannot.
