There is no official price for having a will written. A solicitor sets the fee for taking your instructions, drafting the will and explaining how to sign it. Straightforward wills are usually a fixed fee. VAT is added on top if the firm is registered for VAT. You can [compare will-writing fees](/wills/) with VAT included. The official fees in this guide were checked on 21 November 2026.

A will is valid because it is signed in the right way, where you live. It does not have to be registered with a court. [Wills and powers of attorney across the UK](/blog/wills-and-powers-of-attorney-uk-differences/) sets out the signing rules. A [power of attorney](/blog/power-of-attorney-cost-uk/) is a different document, with its own registration fee.

## What does the solicitor's fee cover?

For a straightforward will, ask the quote to include:

- A meeting or call to take your instructions
- Drafting the will
- At least one round of changes
- Guidance on signing, or a supervised signing appointment
- What happens to the original, and whether storage is included

[Questions to ask before you instruct](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-instructing-a-solicitor/) covers the same checks for any quote: what is inside the fixed fee, what would increase it, and whether VAT is included.

## How much is a mirror will?

A mirror will is not a special legal document. It is two wills, usually for a couple, that reflect each other: each person leaves their estate to the other, and then to the same people. Each of you still has your own will, and either of you can change yours later.

Firms often charge less for the pair than for two unrelated wills, because the instructions are taken together. Ask for the price of the pair and the price of one will on its own, so you can see the difference. If you and your partner want to leave things differently, say so at the start. That is two individual wills, and the quote should say so.

## What makes a will cost more?

The fee rises when the advice takes longer. Common reasons are:

- A trust, for example where children would inherit at a stated age
- A business, a farm, or a share in a partnership
- A home or other property abroad
- A blended family, or a wish to leave a close relative out
- Doubt about whether you can understand and sign the will
- Inheritance tax planning

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, a family member or dependant who is left out can ask a court for reasonable financial provision. The will can still do what you ask, and the solicitor will usually explain how to record your reasons.

Scotland is different. A spouse or civil partner, and children, have **legal rights** to a share of the moveable estate (money, shares and belongings, rather than land and buildings) whatever the will says. A Scottish will needs someone who knows Scots law. You can [compare will-writing fees in Scotland](/wills/scotland/).

## Do the signing rules change the fee?

The solicitor's work follows the rules where you live. The fee itself is still the firm's own price.

| | England and Wales | Scotland | Northern Ireland |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum age | 18 | 12 | 18 |
| Witnesses | Two, present together | One | Two, present together |
| Does marriage cancel an existing will? | Yes, unless it was made in expectation of that marriage | No | Yes, unless it was made in expectation of that marriage |
| Who regulates a solicitor | Solicitors Regulation Authority | Law Society of Scotland | Law Society of Northern Ireland |

Anyone can offer to write a will. A solicitor is regulated, must carry insurance, and you can complain to the Legal Ombudsman in England and Wales, the Scottish Legal Complaints Commission, or the Law Society of Northern Ireland. If you use someone who is not a solicitor, ask who regulates them, whether they are insured, and who puts the will right if it is signed incorrectly.

Some charities run occasional will-writing schemes, sometimes in return for a suggested donation. Check the charity's own page for whether a scheme is open, and whether it covers anything beyond a simple will.

## Is there a fee to store or register a will?

Storage is optional. A will that never reaches your executors is of little use, so it is worth deciding where the original will live and telling them.

In England and Wales you can deposit a will with the Probate Service. HM Courts and Tribunals Service charges a one-off **£24**, by cheque or postal order. If you send the will and any codicil in the same envelope, that is one fee. The service was updated on 13 July 2026. People on a low income or certain benefits can apply for Help with Fees. Many solicitors store wills as well, and that charge is the firm's own.

In Scotland you can register a will in the Register of Deeds, part of the Books of Council and Session, for safekeeping. Registers of Scotland charges **£20**, which includes the first extract. The page stating that fee was updated on 13 November 2026. Registration is voluntary, and a later will can be registered too. If your executors will need the extract for confirmation, ask for a paper extract: the Scottish Courts and Tribunals Service does not accept a digital extract for confirmation.

Northern Ireland has no equivalent public storage fee on the court list. Ask whether the solicitor's quote includes keeping the original.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a solicitor charge for a will?

There is no national fee. Solicitors set their own price, usually a fixed fee for a simple will, with VAT on top if they are registered. A pair of mirror wills is often priced as one job. Trusts, a business, property abroad or a blended family cost more because the advice takes longer. [Compare single-will fees](/wills/) to see firms' own totals.

### Is a will made online valid?

It can be, if you sign it in the way the law requires where you live: two witnesses together in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and one witness in Scotland, with your signature on every page. The risk is a will that is signed properly and still fails to do what you wanted. That risk is higher if your estate includes a business, a trust, or family in Scotland who have legal rights.

### Does a will have to be registered?

No. A will is valid without registration in every part of the UK. Optional storage is £24 with the Probate Service in England and Wales, and £20 to register in the Register of Deeds in Scotland.

### Does a will avoid probate?

A will names your executors and says who inherits. It does not remove the need for probate in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or confirmation in Scotland, when banks or a land registry ask for it. [Probate costs across the UK](/blog/probate-costs-uk/) explains those court fees.
