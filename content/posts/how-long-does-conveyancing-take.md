There is no single answer to how long conveyancing takes, and the published figures measure different things. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/buying-a-home) says buying a home in England and Wales takes about five months on average, and longer if you are in a chain of buyers and sellers. That is the whole purchase, from deciding to buy through to moving in. The [Council for Licensed Conveyancers](https://www.clc-uk.org/faq/), which regulates licensed conveyancers in England and Wales, says that if there are no significant delays the conveyancing transaction itself usually takes 8 to 12 weeks. In Northern Ireland, [nidirect](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/buying-home-step-step-guide) says buying a home usually takes around two to three months. Scotland does not publish an equivalent average: the date you get the keys is the date of entry agreed in the offer, and the contract becomes binding when missives are concluded, which can be well before that date.

None of these is a promise for your move. Chains, searches, leasehold packs and mortgage offers decide the pace. You can [compare conveyancing fees](/conveyancing/) while you choose who will run the file.

## The stages in England and Wales

An accepted offer is not binding. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/buying-a-home/transferring-ownership) says the agreement becomes binding when contracts are exchanged, and that after exchange neither side can usually pull out without paying compensation. The work in between is the conveyancing.

1. **Instruction.** The seller's conveyancer drafts the contract. Yours checks the title and raises enquiries. GOV.UK's selling guidance says the seller should instruct someone shortly before the home is marketed, or as soon as it is on the market, because that saves time later.
2. **Searches and the survey.** Your conveyancer orders the searches. You arrange a survey. If you need a mortgage, you apply and wait for the offer. These three often run together, and any one of them can hold the others.
3. **Exchange.** Both sides sign and the contracts are exchanged. A deposit is paid. The completion date is fixed in the contract.
4. **Completion.** Your conveyancer sends the money, you get the keys, and you own the home.
5. **Registration.** Your conveyancer registers you at HM Land Registry and files the Stamp Duty Land Tax return, which is due within 14 days of completion. In Wales the tax is Land Transaction Tax, due within 30 days.

[Conveyancing costs in England](/blog/conveyancing-costs-england/) and [conveyancing costs in Wales](/blog/conveyancing-costs-wales/) cover what each of those stages costs. The legal steps match in the two countries. The tax does not.

## How long it takes in Scotland

Scottish conveyancing does not use exchange of contracts, and mygov.scot does not give a national average in weeks. The order of events is different enough that an English "8 to 12 weeks after offer" is the wrong yardstick.

- The seller usually has a [Home Report](https://www.mygov.scot/buy-home/home-report) before the home is marketed. You can read the survey before you offer.
- Your solicitor notes your interest. If several people do, the seller may set a closing date.
- The offer is a formal letter from your solicitor, and it states the date you want to collect the keys. That date of entry is negotiated. It is not fixed by a national timetable.
- The letters that follow are the **missives**. When they are concluded you have a binding contract. [mygov.scot](https://www.mygov.scot/buy-home/make-offer) warns that pulling out after that leaves you liable to pay the seller damages.
- [Settlement](https://www.mygov.scot/buy-home/settlement) is the day you get the keys, usually the date of entry. Your solicitor's examination of the title is described on mygov.scot as the conveyancing that follows the concluded missives.

Choose a Scottish solicitor before you start viewing, so they can note interest quickly. [Buying a house in Scotland](/blog/buying-a-house-in-scotland-conveyancing/) explains missives, the Home Report and Land and Buildings Transaction Tax. The solicitor has to be qualified in Scots law.

## How long it takes in Northern Ireland

nidirect's "around two to three months" is the whole of buying a home, and it says you should be ready for delays if you are in a chain. The steps it lists are: decide how you will pay, find a home, make an offer, instruct a solicitor, get a survey, apply for a mortgage if you need one, exchange contracts, then pay and move in on completion.

Exchange is the point the sale becomes binding. nidirect says you will usually pay a deposit of at least 5% of the price at that stage. Your solicitor must be qualified in Northern Ireland and is regulated by the [Law Society of Northern Ireland](https://www.lawsoc-ni.org/). A firm qualified only in England and Wales cannot act. Some older titles are still in the Registry of Deeds rather than Land Registry NI, and that extra reading is one reason a file can take longer. [Conveyancing costs in Northern Ireland](/blog/conveyancing-costs-northern-ireland/) covers the quote and the stamp duty, which matches England.

## What makes a move take longer

The same kinds of delay show up in every nation. The file is waiting on someone else.

- **A chain.** Each sale waits for the next. GOV.UK and nidirect both say this is why a purchase overruns.
- **The mortgage offer**, or a survey that leads to a renegotiation.
- **Searches**, where a council or a water company is slow, or a further search is needed.
- **Leasehold information.** A management pack from a freeholder or agent is a frequent cause of delay in England, Wales and Northern Ireland.
- **An unclear title**, including unregistered land.
- **People.** Signed papers, identity documents and replies to enquiries that sit in an inbox.

No firm can honestly promise a completion date on the day you instruct them. Ask for a working estimate and for what would change it. [Questions to ask before you instruct](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-instructing-a-solicitor/) includes timing, and who will actually reply when you chase.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does conveyancing take in England?

GOV.UK says buying a home in England and Wales takes about five months on average, longer in a chain. That includes finding the home. The Council for Licensed Conveyancers says the legal work itself usually takes 8 to 12 weeks if there are no significant delays. Wales uses the same stages.

### How long does it take to buy a house in Scotland?

There is no official average in weeks. The date of entry is agreed in the offer, and the contract becomes binding when missives are concluded, which can be weeks before you move. Pulling out after that can mean a claim for damages.

### How long does conveyancing take in Northern Ireland?

nidirect says buying a home usually takes around two to three months, and longer if you are in a chain. Your solicitor must be qualified in Northern Ireland.

### Does a chain make conveyancing longer?

Yes. GOV.UK says a purchase in England and Wales can take longer than the average of about five months when you are part of a chain, and nidirect gives the same warning for Northern Ireland.
