There is no official solicitor's fee for extending a lease, and no official premium. In England and Wales the statutory route adds 90 years to the lease of a flat, or 50 years to the lease of a house, if you qualify. The premium you pay the landlord is calculated for that flat or house. It rises sharply once 80 years or fewer remain. Your other costs are your own solicitor and valuer, the landlord's reasonable costs, a Land Registry fee to register the new lease, and a tribunal fee only if you cannot agree. You can [compare conveyancing fees in England](/conveyancing/england/) and [in Wales](/conveyancing/wales/). This route does not apply in Scotland or Northern Ireland.

## What does a lease extension actually buy?

[GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/leasehold-property/extending-changing-or-ending-a-lease) describes the two statutory extensions. A qualifying leaseholder of a flat can claim a new lease 90 years longer than the term that remains, at a peppercorn ground rent, under the Leasehold Reform, Housing and Urban Development Act 1993. A qualifying leaseholder of a house can claim a 50-year extension under the Leasehold Reform Act 1967. The house rules are different from the flat rules, including how the ground rent is reset. The Leasehold Advisory Service publishes the detail.

You can also agree an extension informally with the landlord. An informal deal can be on any terms the landlord offers, which may be shorter, or may keep a ground rent. A solicitor's job is to compare that offer with the statutory terms before you accept it.

You no longer need to have owned the property for two years before you claim. Section 27 of the Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 removed that qualifying period on 31 January 2025. You still have to be a qualifying tenant, and the claim is made by the registered owner. HM Land Registry records the change in [practice guide 27](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-leasehold-reform-legislation-pg27/practice-guide-27-the-leasehold-reform-legislation).

## Which parts of the 2024 Act are not in force yet?

The Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 will, when the relevant sections are brought into force, extend statutory leases by 990 years, remove marriage value from the price, and stop leaseholders paying the freeholder's process costs. The government's [plain-English explainer](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/leasehold-enfranchisement-valuation-rates/plain-english-explainer-extending-your-lease-or-buying-your-freehold-its-getting-cheaper-and-easier) describes those changes as still to come. They need regulations, and a consultation on the valuation rates ran until 21 October 2026. Until they start, budget on the current law:

- The premium still includes marriage value where 80 years or fewer remain. That is why GOV.UK says the cost increases significantly at that point.
- On a statutory claim, the leaseholder still pays the landlord's reasonable valuation and legal costs. Those costs are not on a tariff.
- A flat still gains 90 years, not 990.

Do not agree a deal, or delay one, on the assumption that the new valuation method already applies. Ask the solicitor to say which statute the quote is based on.

## What is in the bill?

Checked 21 November 2026. None of the solicitor, valuer or premium lines is an official fee.

| Part | What it is | Official figure, where there is one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your solicitor | Serves or answers the notice, agrees the draft lease, and registers it. The statutory timetable is strict, so this is rarely a do-it-yourself form. | Set by the firm. Compare the total, not a headline. |
| Your valuer | Calculates, or negotiates, the premium. | A professional fee, not a court fee. |
| The premium | Paid to the landlord for the extra years. | Not a tariff. GOV.UK points to the Leasehold Advisory Service calculator as a guide only, not a quote. |
| The landlord's costs | The landlord's reasonable valuation and legal costs on a statutory claim. | No tariff. Still payable by the leaseholder until the 2024 Act cost rules commence. |
| Tribunal, if you cannot agree | The First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) decides the premium and the terms. | £114 to apply under the 1967 Act or the 1993 Act, and £227 if a hearing is listed. In force since 13 July 2026. |
| HM Land Registry | Registers the new lease. There is no reduced electronic fee for a lease. | Scale 1 on the premium: £45 up to £80,000, £330 from £200,001 to £500,000, and £1,105 over £1 million. |

VAT at 20% is added to your solicitor's fee. A premium can also attract Stamp Duty Land Tax in England, or Land Transaction Tax in Wales, if it is high enough. A peppercorn rent usually means no tax on the rent itself. Many premiums fall inside the nil-rate band. The [stamp duty comparison](/blog/stamp-duty-sdlt-ltt-lbtt-compared/) has the bands, and your conveyancer applies them to the premium rather than to the flat's full market value.

How these lines should appear on a quote is covered in [conveyancing quotes in England and Wales](/blog/conveyancing-quote-explained-england-wales/). The questions to put to the firm are in [questions to ask before you instruct](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-instructing-a-solicitor/).

## How does the statutory route work, in outline?

You, or more often your solicitor, serve a formal notice on the landlord. That starts a timetable. The landlord responds with a counter-notice. If you agree the premium and the wording of the lease, there is no hearing and no hearing fee. If you do not agree, either side can apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber). The application fee is £114. The £227 hearing fee is charged only if a hearing is listed.

Missing a deadline in that timetable can lose the claim. That is the practical reason firms charge more for a statutory extension than for a simple purchase task, and why the notice is not a form to send casually. An informal deal avoids the timetable and the tribunal, but only if the terms are actually better than the statute once the landlord's costs are included.

## What should you ask before you instruct?

- Is the quote for a statutory claim, an informal deal, or both, so you can compare them?
- Does it include your valuer, or only the legal work?
- What estimate has the firm given for the landlord's costs, and what happens if those costs come in higher?
- Is the premium a separate payment to the landlord, not part of the legal fee?
- Will the fee rise if the claim goes to a tribunal hearing?
- Who will be registered as the owner before the notice is served?

A lease extension is often discussed at the same time as a sale or a remortgage, because a short lease puts buyers and lenders off. It is still a separate piece of work from the purchase quote. [Compare conveyancing fees](/conveyancing/) for the sale or purchase itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a solicitor charge to extend a lease?

There is no official fee. You pay your solicitor, your valuer, the premium, and usually the landlord's reasonable costs. Ask for each of those separately, plus VAT and the Land Registry fee. Tribunal fees apply only if the price is not agreed.

### How much is the premium to extend a lease?

It is calculated for the property. It is not a fixed sum, and it rises sharply when 80 years or fewer remain, because marriage value is still part of the price. The Leasehold Advisory Service calculator, which GOV.UK links to, is a guide. It is not the figure the landlord has to accept.

### Do I have to have owned the flat for two years?

No. The two-year qualifying period was removed on 31 January 2025. You still have to qualify as a leaseholder, and the person who claims should be the registered owner.

### Can I extend by 990 years yet?

Not under the statutory right. That part of the Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 is not in force. A statutory flat extension is still 90 years on top of what remains, at a peppercorn rent. A statutory house extension is 50 years.

### What if we cannot agree the price?

Either side can apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber). The application fee is £114, and a hearing fee of £227 is payable if a hearing is listed. Both have applied since 13 July 2026. Many claims settle without a hearing.

### Does this apply in Scotland or Northern Ireland?

No. This guide is England and Wales only. Scottish and Northern Ireland property law is different. [Conveyancing costs in Northern Ireland](/blog/conveyancing-costs-northern-ireland/) covers long leases and fee farm grants there.
