The solicitor's fee for selling a house is a price the firm sets. There is no national scale for it. On top of the fee you pay VAT at 20%, and a shorter list of disbursements than the buyer pays. You do not pay Stamp Duty Land Tax, Land Transaction Tax or Land and Buildings Transaction Tax on the sale. Those are the buyer's taxes. In England and Wales you do not normally pay for the searches either. The buyer commissions those. You can [compare conveyancing fees](/conveyancing/) for a sale with VAT and disbursements in the total.

[GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/selling-a-home) says that to sell a home in England and Wales you need a solicitor or conveyancer to do the legal work of transferring ownership, alongside the estate agent and an Energy Performance Certificate. Selling takes about five months on average, and longer in a chain. [How long conveyancing takes](/blog/how-long-does-conveyancing-take/) separates that figure from the legal work itself.

## What a sale quote should include

| Part of the quote | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Legal fee | Drafting the contract, replying to the buyer's enquiries, exchanging or concluding the bargain, and sending you the proceeds |
| VAT at 20% | Charged on the legal fee. Check the quote shows it |
| Official copies | Proof of the title. In England and Wales, £7 each electronically for the register and for the title plan |
| Identity checks | Required for the seller. Charged by the firm, with no official tariff |
| Bank transfer | Sending the proceeds to you, and redeeming a mortgage if you have one |
| Leasehold pack, if it applies | A fee set by the freeholder or managing agent, not by a national scale |
| Tax on the sale | You do not pay SDLT, LTT or LBTT for selling |

The buyer's searches, the fee to register the buyer, and the buyer's tax are on the buyer's quote. [Conveyancing disbursements explained](/blog/conveyancing-disbursements-explained/) sets out those official fees. [How to read a conveyancing quote](/blog/conveyancing-quote-explained-england-wales/) is the matching guide for England and Wales.

Firms regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority must publish residential sale prices and say what is included. If an estate agent referred you, both the agent and the firm should tell you about any referral fee. You do not have to use the firm they suggest. [Questions to ask before you instruct](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-instructing-a-solicitor/) includes that, and what you would pay if the sale fell through.

## Official copies, not a search pack

In England and Wales the seller's conveyancer orders official copies so the contract can be drafted from the register. Under [HM Land Registry's information services fees](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/hm-land-registry-information-services-fees), in force since 9 December 2024, an official copy of the register is **£7** electronically and **£11** by post, and the title plan is the same price again. A typical sale needs both.

The fee to register the buyer as the new owner is Scale 1, and it is the buyer's disbursement. [Conveyancing costs in England](/blog/conveyancing-costs-england/) has that scale. It is the same in Wales, because Wales uses HM Land Registry too.

## Leasehold and other extras

If you are selling a leasehold flat or house, the freeholder or managing agent usually charges for a management pack and for notice of the transfer. The lease sets the notice fee. There is no official national price, and the pack is a common reason a sale quote is higher than the headline fee, and a common reason the sale slows down. Ask whether the number in the quote is the actual fee or an estimate, and whether you or the buyer will pay it.

An unregistered title means older deeds rather than an online register, which is more work. Selling and buying at the same time is two files, even when one firm does both. Redeeming a mortgage is part of a normal sale. The lender may charge its own exit fee. That comes out of the proceeds. It is not the conveyancer's fee.

## England and Wales

[GOV.UK's guide to selling](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-to-sell-a-home/how-to-sell-a-home) says you should instruct a conveyancer shortly before you put the home on the market, or as soon as it is up for sale. Early instruction gives them time to order the title and prepare the contract pack, which is what the buyer's conveyancer will raise enquiries on.

You must have an [Energy Performance Certificate](https://www.gov.uk/selling-a-home/energy-performance-certificates) before you market, unless a valid one already exists or the home is exempt. A certificate lasts 10 years. An accredited assessor sets the price. It is often arranged by the estate agent, and it is separate from the solicitor's fee. You can be fined if you market without one when you need one.

The offer is not binding. The sale becomes binding on exchange of contracts. On completion your conveyancer pays off your mortgage, pays the estate agent's bill if that has been agreed, takes their own fees, and sends you the balance. The buyer pays Stamp Duty Land Tax in England, or Land Transaction Tax in Wales. [Conveyancing costs in Wales](/blog/conveyancing-costs-wales/) explains that tax.

A solicitor or a licensed conveyancer can act. Both are regulated. You can use a firm anywhere in England or Wales.

## Scotland

The seller's costs start earlier. [mygov.scot](https://www.mygov.scot/sell-home/first-steps) says the first step is a Home Report: a single survey and valuation, a property questionnaire, and an energy report. If you do not give it to an interested buyer within nine days of being asked, the council's trading standards service can fine you £500. Some homes are exempt, including a new house sold off-plan to the first occupier. The surveyor's fee is the seller's cost. It is not a solicitor's disbursement, and there is no official tariff for it.

You still need an Energy Performance Certificate. In Scotland that sits inside the Home Report, and [mygov.scot](https://www.mygov.scot/energy-performance-certificates/when-you-need-epc) says selling without one when you need one can mean a fine of at least £500.

Your solicitor sends the contract letters, called missives. The sale is binding when missives are concluded, which can be well before the date of entry. Many Scottish solicitors also act as estate agents, so one firm may be doing both jobs. They are different fees. The solicitor must be qualified in Scots law. The fee to register the buyer's title at Registers of Scotland is the buyer's cost. [Buying a house in Scotland](/blog/buying-a-house-in-scotland-conveyancing/) explains missives and Land and Buildings Transaction Tax, which the buyer pays.

## Northern Ireland

Your solicitor prepares the contract and replies to the buyer's solicitor. The sale becomes binding on exchange, and completion is the day the buyer pays and can move in. [nidirect](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/buying-home-step-step-guide) describes those steps from the buyer's side and says a purchase usually takes around two to three months, longer in a chain.

The solicitor must be qualified in Northern Ireland. A firm from England cannot sell a Northern Ireland home for you. The buyer pays Stamp Duty Land Tax at England's rates. Registration is at Land Registry NI, or in the Registry of Deeds if the title is still unregistered, which can mean more work on the sale as well as the purchase. [Conveyancing costs in Northern Ireland](/blog/conveyancing-costs-northern-ireland/) covers that.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a solicitor charge to sell a house?

The firm sets the fee. Add VAT at 20% and the disbursements, which for a sale are mainly official copies, identity checks and the bank transfer. There is no official UK scale for the legal fee, so compare totals. Leasehold packs and unregistered titles cost extra.

### Do I pay stamp duty when I sell my house?

No. Stamp Duty Land Tax, Land Transaction Tax and Land and Buildings Transaction Tax are charged to the buyer. Your conveyancer does not collect them from you on a sale.

### Do I pay for searches when I sell?

In England and Wales the buyer commissions the searches. You pay for official copies of the register and the title plan, at £7 each if they are ordered electronically. In Scotland you pay for the Home Report before you market.

### Do I need a solicitor to sell in Scotland?

Yes. A solicitor qualified in Scots law deals with the missives, and the contract is binding once they are concluded. The Home Report is a separate cost, paid to a surveyor, before the home is marketed.
