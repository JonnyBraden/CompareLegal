There is no official scale for what a solicitor charges to buy or sell a house in Northern Ireland. The figure that matters is the total: the firm's legal fee, VAT at 20% on that fee, and outlays such as searches and Land Registry fees. Stamp Duty Land Tax is collected as well, and it is the same whichever firm you use. Your solicitor must be qualified in Northern Ireland. You can [compare conveyancing fees in Northern Ireland](/conveyancing/northern-ireland/) with VAT and outlays in the total.

## What does a Northern Ireland conveyancing quote include?

A complete quote has three parts. Compare the **total**, not the headline legal fee.

| Part of the quote | What it is | Official figure, checked 21 November 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal fee | The firm's own work: title, enquiries, reporting to you and your lender, completion and registration. Most firms quote a fixed fee that rises with the price. | Set by the firm. There is no Northern Ireland scale. |
| VAT | Charged on the legal fee. | 20%, the standard rate. |
| Outlays | Costs the firm pays to others for you, including searches, a priority search and Land Registry fees. | Land Registry scale below. Search fees vary and should be itemised. |
| Stamp Duty Land Tax | A tax on the purchase, usually paid through your solicitor. Not a legal fee. | Same bands as England. See below. |

The [guide to conveyancing quotes in England and Wales](/blog/conveyancing-quote-explained-england-wales/) covers that system. The checks to make before you instruct anyone are in [questions to ask a solicitor](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-instructing-a-solicitor/).

## What are the Land Registry fees?

Most titles are registered at Land Registry NI, part of Land & Property Services. The fee to register a transfer, lease or change of ownership depends on the value of the estate and on whether the application is lodged electronically. The scale is in the Land Registry (Fees) Order (Northern Ireland) 2014, still the order in force when checked on 21 November 2026. The Department of Finance consulted in 2026 on changing the bands. Until a new order is made, these are the fees.

| Value of the estate | Fee | Electronic lodgement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| £20,000 or less | £100 | £80 |
| £20,001 to £80,000 | £160 | £120 |
| £80,001 to £100,000 | £220 | £160 |
| £100,001 to £150,000 | £260 | £220 |
| £150,001 to £200,000 | £370 | £310 |
| £200,001 to £250,000 | £425 | £355 |
| Over £250,000 | £535 | £445 |

Other fees in the same order, which a quote should list when they apply:

- **Registering a charge** (the mortgage): £110, or £90 if lodged electronically.
- **Releasing a charge** when a mortgage is paid off: £20.
- **A priority search**, including the certificate: £31.

Where a registered owner transfers the land to themselves and another person, the fee is worked out on the share passing to the other person. Where registered co-owners transfer the land to themselves, the minimum fee applies (£100, or £80 electronically). [Transfer of equity solicitor fees](/blog/transfer-of-equity-solicitor-fees/) explains that job.

Some older titles are still unregistered and recorded in the Registry of Deeds. Reading through older deeds is extra work, so expect a higher fee or a separate charge.

## How much is stamp duty in Northern Ireland?

Northern Ireland uses Stamp Duty Land Tax at the same rates as England. From 1 April 2025, SDLT on a main home is:

| Part of the price | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to £125,000 | 0% |
| £125,001 to £250,000 | 2% |
| £250,001 to £925,000 | 5% |
| £925,001 to £1.5 million | 10% |
| Over £1.5 million | 12% |

First-time buyers pay nothing on the first £300,000 and 5% on the portion between £300,001 and £500,000, if the price is not over £500,000. Above £500,000 the relief is lost and the normal rates apply. Buying an additional property adds a further percentage to each band. The [comparison of SDLT, LTT and LBTT](/blog/stamp-duty-sdlt-ltt-lbtt-compared/) sets the three UK taxes side by side, and [HMRC's calculator](https://www.tax.service.gov.uk/calculate-stamp-duty-land-tax/) gives a figure for a particular price.

Many homes in Northern Ireland sell for less than the UK average, so plenty of buyers pay little or no stamp duty. That makes the legal fee and outlays a larger share of the moving cost, and worth comparing.

## How is conveyancing in Northern Ireland different?

- **A separate profession.** Your solicitor must be qualified in Northern Ireland and is regulated by the [Law Society of Northern Ireland](https://www.lawsoc-ni.org/). A firm qualified only in England and Wales cannot act on an NI property.
- **Two land registers.** Registered titles are at Land Registry NI. Unregistered titles are in the Registry of Deeds.
- **Different searches.** These include property certificates from the council and statutory bodies, and a search against the seller in the Enforcement of Judgments Office. They are outlays and should be itemised. There is no single national price for a property certificate.
- **The same stamp duty as England**, unlike Scotland and Wales, which have their own property taxes.

## What makes the solicitor's fee higher?

- **Leasehold, or a fee farm grant.** Many homes, especially in Belfast, are held on long leases or fee farm grants with a ground rent. Checking the title and getting management information adds work. The statutory lease-extension route used in England and Wales does not apply here. That process is explained in [lease extension solicitor fees](/blog/lease-extension-solicitor-fees/).
- **New builds.** Developers set tight deadlines and there is extra paperwork for the new title. [Shared ownership and new-build costs](/blog/shared-ownership-and-new-build-conveyancing-costs/) sets out why those quotes are higher.
- **Unregistered title**, as above.
- **Co-Ownership.** Buying through Co-Ownership means dealing with a third party as well as the seller. The same guide explains the extra paperwork. The SDLT and Land Registry figures on this page still apply to the price you pay.
- **A lender's own requirements**, which some firms charge for separately.
- **A change of owners** at the same time as the purchase or remortgage. That is a transfer of equity.

Ask each firm whether its fee is fixed, and what would make it rise, before you instruct them. [Compare the totals](/conveyancing/northern-ireland/) rather than the headline fee.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does conveyancing cost in Northern Ireland?

There is no official fee. You pay the solicitor's legal fee, VAT at 20% on that fee, and outlays such as searches and Land Registry fees. Buyers also pay Stamp Duty Land Tax, which does not change with the firm. Compare the total.

### How long does conveyancing take in Northern Ireland?

A straightforward purchase usually takes 8 to 12 weeks from offer to completion. Chains, mortgage delays and unregistered titles can make it longer.

### Can I use a solicitor from England for a house in Belfast?

No. The solicitor must be qualified in Northern Ireland, because the property law and the land registers are different. Any NI solicitor can act for you, wherever in Northern Ireland their office is.

### Do I pay stamp duty in Northern Ireland?

Yes. Northern Ireland uses Stamp Duty Land Tax at the same rates as England, including first-time buyer relief on homes up to £500,000. The tax is the same whichever solicitor you instruct.

### What does Land Registry NI charge to register a purchase?

It depends on the value and on whether the application is lodged electronically. On the 2014 scale, an electronic application is £220 where the value is £100,001 to £150,000, and £445 where it is over £250,000. A mortgage charge is a further £90 if lodged electronically, or £110 otherwise.

### Who regulates conveyancing solicitors in Northern Ireland?

The Law Society of Northern Ireland. A firm qualified only in England and Wales, or a licensed conveyancer in England and Wales, cannot act on an NI property.
