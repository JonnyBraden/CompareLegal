The court fee and the solicitor's fee are different things, and only the court fee is fixed. In England and Wales, applying for probate costs £526 if the estate is worth more than £5,000, and nothing if it is worth £5,000 or less. That fee has applied since 13 July 2026. Scotland does not call this probate: executors apply for **confirmation**, and the fee depends on the value of the estate. Northern Ireland has its own grant fee. On top of whichever fee applies, a solicitor may charge for a grant-only service or for winding up the whole estate. [Compare probate fees](/probate/) for either level of work. VAT and disbursements should be in the total.

## What are the official court and confirmation fees?

Checked 21 November 2026.

| | What you apply for | Official fee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| England and Wales | Grant of probate, or letters of administration if there is no will | £526 if the estate is worth more than £5,000. No fee at £5,000 or less. In force since 13 July 2026. |
| Scotland | Confirmation, after the sheriff court examines the inventory | No fee up to £50,000. £351 from £50,000.01 to £250,000. £705 above £250,000. In force since 1 April 2026. |
| Northern Ireland | Grant of probate, or letters of administration | No fee if the assessed value is £10,000 or less. £326 if it is more. A personal applicant pays a further £81 when the estate is over £10,000. In force since 1 April 2026. |

In England and Wales, extra copies of the grant cost £2 each if you order them with the application, and £16 each later. A second grant for the same estate costs £22. In Scotland, a certificate of confirmation ordered with the inventory is £10. None of these figures is the solicitor's fee.

You can apply yourself in all three systems. People on a low income may be able to get help with the England and Wales fee. The solicitor's charge, if you use one, is not covered by that help.

## How does each system work?

### England and Wales

Executors apply to HM Courts and Tribunals Service, usually online. If inheritance tax forms are needed, they go to HMRC first. You can apply yourself, pay a solicitor to obtain the grant only, or ask them to administer the whole estate. Firms regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority must publish prices for uncontested probate where the assets are in the UK, which makes a grant-only fixed fee easier to compare.

### Scotland

There is no probate. Executors apply to the sheriff court for confirmation, using an inventory of the estate. Winding the estate up is called executry. Estates at or below the small-estate limit can use a simpler procedure, and the sheriff clerk's office can help with the forms. The limit and the steps are on the [Scottish Courts and Tribunals Service](https://www.scotcourts.gov.uk/taking-action/dealing-with-a-deceaseds-estate-in-scotland) page. You can [compare confirmation and probate fees](/probate/) across the UK, including firms in Scotland.

### Northern Ireland

Applications go to the Probate Office at the Royal Courts of Justice in Belfast, which issues grants for the whole of Northern Ireland. You can apply yourself through [nidirect](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/probate) or instruct a solicitor. Who inherits when there is no will also differs from England and Wales. [Wills and powers of attorney across the UK](/blog/wills-and-powers-of-attorney-uk-differences/) sets out those differences.

## What do solicitors charge, on top of the court fee?

Firms usually offer two levels of work. Neither has an official price.

- **Grant or confirmation only.** The firm prepares the papers and obtains the grant or confirmation. You then collect the assets and pay the beneficiaries. This is often a fixed fee. Ask whether VAT, the court fee and copies are included or on top.
- **Full estate administration.** The firm values the assets, deals with banks and pension providers, sells or transfers property, pays debts and tax, and distributes the estate. Firms charge a fixed fee, an hourly rate, or a percentage of the estate. A percentage can grow quickly on a larger estate, so ask for an estimate in pounds and for what would make it rise. There is no official "typical percentage".

VAT at 20% is charged on the legal fee. Disbursements sit on top: the court or confirmation fee, copies, valuations, and notices in The Gazette. Before you instruct, use [questions to ask a solicitor](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-instructing-a-solicitor/). If a home has to be sold, that is a separate conveyancing job: [compare conveyancing fees](/conveyancing/) as well as the grant.

## What about inheritance tax?

Inheritance tax applies across the UK, with the same main rules in England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. Most estates pay none, because of the nil-rate band, the residence nil-rate band when a home passes to children or grandchildren, and the exemption for gifts to a spouse or civil partner. The current bands are on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax). If tax is due, some of it usually has to be paid before the grant or confirmation is issued. That payment is tax, not a legal fee.

## When might you not need a grant or confirmation?

You may not need one if the estate is small, or if everything was owned jointly and passes to the survivor. Each bank sets its own limit for releasing money without a grant, so ask each one. A home owned in the sole name of the person who died almost always needs a grant or confirmation before it can be sold or transferred.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does probate cost in 2026?

In England and Wales the court fee is £526 for estates over £5,000, and nothing at £5,000 or less, from 13 July 2026. In Scotland the confirmation fee is nothing up to £50,000, £351 up to £250,000 and £705 above that, from 1 April 2026. Northern Ireland charges nothing up to £10,000 and £326 above that. A solicitor's fee is extra.

### Is probate cheaper in Scotland?

The court fee can be, and the document is called confirmation, not probate. A Scottish estate of £200,000 pays £351 for the inventory to be examined. The same value pays £526 in England and Wales. Above £250,000, Scotland charges £705. The solicitor's fee is separate in both places.

### What is the probate fee in Northern Ireland?

From 1 April 2026 the grant fee is £326 if the estate is worth more than £10,000, and nothing if it is £10,000 or less. Someone applying without a solicitor pays a further £81 when the estate is over £10,000. Solicitor's fees are on top.

### Can I do probate myself?

Yes, in all three systems. It makes most sense for a simple estate with a will, a few bank accounts and no inheritance tax to pay. Consider a solicitor if there is a property to sell, a business, assets abroad, or a dispute.

### Do solicitors have to charge a percentage of the estate?

No. A percentage is one way of charging for full administration. Firms also offer fixed fees and hourly rates. For a grant only, a fixed fee is common in England and Wales, where firms must publish prices for uncontested probate. Ask for the estimate in pounds.

### Is the court fee included in a solicitor's quote?

Not always. Ask whether the quote is the legal fee alone, or the legal fee plus VAT, the court or confirmation fee, and copies. Compare those totals, not the headline fee.
