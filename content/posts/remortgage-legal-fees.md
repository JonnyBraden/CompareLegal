If you're moving your mortgage to a new lender, someone has to do the legal work: paying off the old mortgage and registering the new lender's charge against your home. It's simpler than buying a house, and often cheaper, but there are a few choices that affect what you pay.

## Do you need a solicitor at all?

**Not if you're staying with your lender.** A product transfer, where you move to a new deal with your existing lender, usually needs no legal work, because the lender's charge is already registered.

**Yes if you're switching lenders.** The new lender needs its mortgage registered against your property, and the old mortgage paid off and removed. A solicitor or licensed conveyancer does this, usually acting for both you and the new lender.

## Free legal packages from lenders

Many lenders offer "free legals" on a remortgage, using a firm from their own panel, or offer cashback towards your costs instead. Free legals can be good value for a straightforward remortgage. Before you accept, check:

- **What isn't covered.** Free packages usually cover the standard work only. Extras, such as a transfer of equity or a leasehold property, are often charged separately.
- **The service.** Panel firms handle high volumes. If you'd rather have a named contact or a local firm, paying a modest fee may be worth it.
- **Whether a better rate comes without free legals.** Sometimes the deal with no extras has a lower interest rate that saves more over the term than the legal fee.

## What a remortgage quote should include

- **The legal fee** for acting for you and the lender
- **VAT** at 20% on the fee
- **Registration fees** for the new lender's charge at the land register
- **Searches or search insurance.** Many lenders accept insurance instead of full searches on a remortgage, which is cheaper and quicker.
- **Identity checks and a bank transfer fee**

Ask whether the old lender charges a fee to close your mortgage account, as that comes from your own funds, not the new lender's.

## Remortgaging and changing who owns the home

If someone is being added to or removed from the title at the same time, for example after a marriage or a separation, that's a **transfer of equity**. It means extra work and a higher fee. Depending on the amount of mortgage debt taken on, it can also mean paying property transaction tax, so ask your conveyancer to check before you go ahead.

## How it differs across the UK

- **England and Wales:** the new mortgage is registered at HM Land Registry.
- **Scotland:** a mortgage is called a **standard security** and is registered with Registers of Scotland. Your solicitor must be qualified in Scots law.
- **Northern Ireland:** the mortgage is registered at Land Registry NI, or in the Registry of Deeds for unregistered titles, which can take longer. Your solicitor must be qualified in Northern Ireland.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a remortgage take?

Usually 3 to 5 weeks once the lender has made its mortgage offer, longer if there's a transfer of equity or an unregistered title.

### Is it cheaper to use the lender's free legal service?

Often, for a simple remortgage. Compare the total cost, including any extras and the interest rate on the deal, rather than the legal fee alone.

### Do I pay stamp duty when I remortgage?

Not on a straightforward remortgage. A transfer of equity can trigger it, depending on the share of the mortgage the new owner takes on.
