England and Northern Ireland use Stamp Duty Land Tax, which HMRC collects. Wales uses Land Transaction Tax, collected by the Welsh Revenue Authority, and Scotland uses Land and Buildings Transaction Tax, collected by Revenue Scotland.

The same price is not the same bill. The tax does not change with the firm, and it is not the legal fee. Your solicitor or conveyancer normally files the return and pays it from your money. Compare the legal work on its own: [England](/blog/conveyancing-costs-england/), [Wales](/blog/conveyancing-costs-wales/), [Scotland](/blog/conveyancing-costs-scotland/), [Northern Ireland](/blog/conveyancing-costs-northern-ireland/), or [compare conveyancing fees](/conveyancing/).

## Which property tax applies in each UK nation?

England and Northern Ireland share SDLT. Wales and Scotland each set their own tax. Figures checked November 2026.

| | England and Northern Ireland | Wales | Scotland |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tax | Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) | Land Transaction Tax (LTT) | Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) |
| Authority | HMRC | Welsh Revenue Authority | Revenue Scotland |
| Who files | Buyer, via a conveyancer | Buyer, via a conveyancer | Buyer, via their agent |
| Nil-rate band, main home | £125,000 | £225,000 | £145,000 |
| First-time buyer relief | £0 on the first £300,000 if the price is £500,000 or less | None | Nil-rate band rises to £175,000, saving up to £600 |
| Another home | 5% on top of each SDLT rate | Higher rates, from 5% on the first £180,000 | 8% of the price, on top of LBTT |
| Deadline | 14 days from completion | 30 days from the day after completion | 30 days from the day after the effective date |

## What are the rates on a main home?

Each rate applies only to the slice of the price in that band.

### England and Northern Ireland

Rates where this will be your only residential property, from [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates). Checked November 2026.

| Part of the price | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to £125,000 | 0% |
| £125,001 to £250,000 | 2% |
| £250,001 to £925,000 | 5% |
| £925,001 to £1.5 million | 10% |
| Above £1.5 million | 12% |

GOV.UK's example of a house bought in April 2025 for £295,000 is **£4,750**.

### Wales

[Main residential rates](https://www.gov.wales/land-transaction-tax-rates-and-bands) for an effective date on or after 10 October 2022. The [draft budget of 17 November 2026](https://www.gov.wales/written-statement-draft-budget-2027-28-devolved-welsh-taxes) leaves these rates and thresholds unchanged for 2027-28. There is [no first-time buyers' relief](https://www.gov.wales/land-transaction-tax-overview). Checked November 2026.

| Part of the price | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to and including £225,000 | 0% |
| Over £225,000 up to and including £400,000 | 6% |
| Over £400,000 up to and including £750,000 | 7.5% |
| Over £750,000 up to and including £1,500,000 | 10% |
| Over £1,500,000 | 12% |

The published example of a house at £280,000 is **£3,300**.

### Scotland

[Residential bands](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/residential-property) in place since 1 April 2021. The [Scottish Government](https://www.gov.scot/policies/taxes/land-and-buildings-transaction-tax/) says the 2026-27 Budget left these bands, and the Additional Dwelling Supplement, unchanged. Checked November 2026.

| Part of the price | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to £145,000 | 0% |
| £145,001 to £250,000 | 2% |
| £250,001 to £325,000 | 5% |
| £325,001 to £750,000 | 10% |
| Over £750,000 | 12% |

Revenue Scotland's example of a purchase at £235,000 is **£1,800**.

Added from those slices, a £300,000 main home is **£5,000** in England and Northern Ireland, **£4,500** in Wales and **£4,600** in Scotland (£2,500 plus £2,500; 6% of £75,000; 2% of £105,000 plus 5% of £50,000).

## Do first-time buyers pay stamp duty?

Every buyer must qualify, and the home must be your residence. The legal fee does not change: see [first-time buyer conveyancing costs](/blog/first-time-buyer-conveyancing-costs/).

In England and Northern Ireland, [first-time buyers' relief](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/stamp-duty-land-tax-relief-for-land-or-property-transactions) is no SDLT on the first £300,000 and 5% from £300,001 to £500,000. Above £500,000 the ordinary rates apply to the whole price. A £500,000 first home in GOV.UK's example is **£10,000**. At £300,000 or less, SDLT is £0. Checked November 2026.

Wales has no first-time buyers' relief. The £225,000 nil-rate band applies to every buyer of a main home.

In Scotland, [first-time buyer relief](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/lbtt-legislation-guidance/lbtt3001-exemptions-reliefs/lbtt3010-tax-reliefs/lbtt3048-first-time-buyer-relief) lifts the nil-rate band from £145,000 to £175,000, saving up to **£600**, with no £500,000 cap. It is unavailable if the Additional Dwelling Supplement is due. A £300,000 first home is **£4,000** on these bands, £600 less.

## What if you already own a home?

A second home or a rental costs more than a move from one main home to another. Ask your conveyancer which case you are in before you are committed.

In England and Northern Ireland, [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax/residential-property-rates) adds **5%** to each SDLT rate if you will own more than one residential property. The extra 5% is due if the old main home is unsold at completion, and it can be reclaimed if you sell within 36 months. A buyer not present in the UK for at least 183 days in the previous 12 months usually pays a further **2%** in England or Northern Ireland. Checked November 2026.

In Wales, [higher rates](https://www.gov.wales/land-transaction-tax-rates-and-bands) replace the main rates from 11 December 2024, in slices: 5% to £180,000, 8.5% to £250,000, 10% to £400,000, 12.5% to £750,000, 15% to £1,500,000 and 17% above that. A published second home at £260,000 is **£15,950**. Replacing your main residence can take you off these rates. Checked November 2026.

In Scotland the [Additional Dwelling Supplement](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/additional-dwelling-supplement-ads) is **8%** of the price from 5 December 2024, on top of LBTT. It is not due below £40,000, nor, from 1 April 2024, if you sold your only or main home in the previous 36 months and this purchase replaces it. Supplement already paid can be reclaimed if that sale is within 36 months. On £300,000 the supplement is **£24,000**, and LBTT is charged as well. Checked November 2026.

## When must the return be filed and paid?

The buyer is responsible. The solicitor or conveyancer normally files the return and pays the tax from money you send before completion. That payment is separate from the legal fee, and the same for every firm.

England and Northern Ireland: pay HMRC within **14 days of completion**. A return is still due at [£40,000 or more](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-send-a-stamp-duty-land-tax-return) if no tax is payable. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/stamp-duty-land-tax) says the solicitor, agent or conveyancer usually files and pays on completion. Checked November 2026.

Wales: pay the Welsh Revenue Authority within **30 days of the day after completion**. Your conveyancer can file and pay. A freehold under £40,000 needs no return. Checked November 2026 on the [LTT overview](https://www.gov.wales/land-transaction-tax-overview).

Scotland: pay Revenue Scotland within **30 days of the day after the effective date**, usually completion. The agent files in almost every case, and the buyer remains legally responsible. A return is due at [£40,000 or more](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/residential-property) even when no tax is payable. Checked November 2026 on [Revenue Scotland](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax/lbtt-legislation-guidance/lbtt4001-paying-submitting-lbtt-return/lbtt4002-making-lbtt-return/lbtt4004-duty-make-lbtt-return).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Northern Ireland use Stamp Duty Land Tax?

Yes. Northern Ireland pays SDLT to HMRC, on England's bands, with the same first-time buyer relief and the same 14-day deadline. Wales uses Land Transaction Tax. Scotland uses LBTT.

### Do first-time buyers pay stamp duty?

In England and Northern Ireland, nothing is due on the first £300,000 and 5% applies up to £500,000. Above £500,000 there is no relief. In Scotland the nil-rate band rises from £145,000 to £175,000, saving up to £600, if every buyer qualifies. Wales has no first-time buyer relief: the £225,000 band applies to every main-home buyer.

### Who pays, the buyer or the seller?

The buyer pays, on a purchase of property or land. The seller does not pay these taxes on the sale. A remortgage that leaves the owner unchanged is not a purchase.

### Does my solicitor file the return?

Usually, yes. The conveyancer files and pays HMRC in England and Northern Ireland, the Welsh Revenue Authority in Wales, and Revenue Scotland in Scotland. You remain responsible for an accurate return. The tax is separate from the legal fee.

### When is the return due?

Within 14 days of completion in England and Northern Ireland. Within 30 days of the day after completion in Wales, and within 30 days of the day after the effective date in Scotland. The firm asks you for the money before then.

### Is the charge for a second home the same in each nation?

No. England and Northern Ireland add 5% to each SDLT rate. Wales uses higher rates starting at 5% on the first £180,000. Scotland adds 8% of the price on top of LBTT.
