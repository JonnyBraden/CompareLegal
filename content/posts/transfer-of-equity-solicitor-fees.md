A transfer of equity adds someone to the title, or takes someone off it, without a sale on the open market. There is no official solicitor's fee. You pay the firm's legal fee, VAT at 20%, and a land-registry fee. You may also pay Stamp Duty Land Tax, Land Transaction Tax or Land and Buildings Transaction Tax, but only on the chargeable consideration: cash that changes hands, plus the share of any mortgage the incoming owner takes on. A gift with no mortgage and no cash is not normally taxed. You can [compare conveyancing fees](/conveyancing/) with VAT and disbursements in the total. If the mortgage is moving lender at the same time, read [remortgage legal fees](/blog/remortgage-legal-fees/) as well.

## What are you paying for?

The legal work is more than a name change. The conveyancer checks the title, prepares the transfer, gets the lender's consent if there is a mortgage, and registers the new owners. If someone is coming off the mortgage, the lender has to agree to release them. If someone is joining, the lender usually reassesses the loan.

On a separation, one firm often cannot act for both of you, because your interests differ. Budget for two sets of advice in that case. Where you are adding a partner and you both want the same outcome, one firm may be able to act, but it should say so plainly. [Questions to ask before you instruct](/blog/questions-to-ask-before-instructing-a-solicitor/) includes who the firm is acting for.

## What is in the quote?

Checked 21 November 2026.

| Part | What it is | Official figure, where there is one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal fee | The transfer, the lender's consent, and registration. Higher than a standard remortgage because the owners change. | Set by the firm. |
| VAT | On the legal fee. | 20%. |
| Land registry fee | Registering the new ownership. In England and Wales the fee depends on whether money is paid and on the value used in the fee order. | HM Land Registry Scale 1 or Scale 2. An official example is below. |
| Lender fees | The existing lender may charge to add or remove a borrower, separate from the legal fee. | The lender's tariff. |
| Property tax | SDLT in England and Northern Ireland, LTT in Wales, LBTT in Scotland, on the chargeable consideration if the rules apply. | Not a legal fee. The same whichever firm you use. |
| Identity checks and a bank transfer | Per person, plus sending any money. | Set by the firm. |

In England and Wales the Land Registration Fee Order 2024, in force since 9 December 2024, uses Scale 2 where the transfer is not for money, and the fee can be assessed on the value after deducting the mortgage. HM Land Registry's own example is a £250,000 home with a £100,000 mortgage, transferred to one owner by court order on divorce, with £50,000 paid to the other. The registration fee is worked out on £75,000 and charged on Scale 2. The cash paid is ignored for the fee. Scale 2 for an electronic application over the whole of a registered title is £20 on a value up to £100,000. A postal application in that band is £45. If you are paying a price in the ordinary way, Scale 1 can apply instead. Use the [fee tables](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/hm-land-registry-registration-services-fees), or ask the conveyancer which scale the quote assumes. A consultation on simplifying Scale 2 runs until 22 December 2026 and had not replaced these figures when checked.

## When is tax due on a transfer of equity?

In England and Northern Ireland the tax is Stamp Duty Land Tax. [HMRC's guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/sdlt-transferring-ownership-of-land-or-property) calls anything of monetary value you give the "chargeable consideration". That includes cash you pay the other owner, and the share of the outstanding mortgage you agree to take on, even if no cash changes hands. The worked examples on that page use the thresholds in force when it was last updated, in September 2022. Use the current residential rates, or [HMRC's calculator](https://www.tax.service.gov.uk/calculate-stamp-duty-land-tax/).

On the main-home rates in force since 1 April 2025, chargeable consideration of £200,000 is £1,500: nothing on the first £125,000 and 2% on the remaining £75,000. That illustration assumes this is your only home. If you already own another dwelling, higher rates can apply. The [comparison of SDLT, LTT and LBTT](/blog/stamp-duty-sdlt-ltt-lbtt-compared/) sets those rates out.

Three situations come up often:

- **A gift, and no mortgage.** If nothing is paid and nobody takes on a debt, SDLT is not normally due.
- **A gift, but the recipient takes on a mortgage.** SDLT can be due if the debt taken on is over the threshold. Taking on half of a £300,000 mortgage is £150,000 of chargeable consideration, before any cash is added.
- **Divorce, dissolution or separation.** You do not pay SDLT, and you do not need to tell HMRC, if you transfer a share to a spouse or civil partner under an agreement or a court order because you are divorcing, dissolving a civil partnership, annulling the marriage or legally separating. The court fee and the solicitor's fee for the divorce are a different bill: [divorce costs](/blog/divorce-costs-uk/).

Wales charges Land Transaction Tax and Scotland charges LBTT. The idea of chargeable consideration is similar, but the bands and the reliefs are not the English ones. Do not assume the divorce exemption above applies word for word. Ask the conveyancer to check the [Welsh Revenue Authority](https://www.gov.wales/land-transaction-tax-guide) or [Revenue Scotland](https://revenue.scot/taxes/land-buildings-transaction-tax) guidance before you treat the transfer as tax-free.

Whether an SDLT return is required, even when no tax is due, depends on the transfer. The conveyancer files it when it is needed. That filing is not itself a reason to prefer one firm on price.

## How does the paperwork differ across the UK?

- **England and Wales.** The transfer is registered at HM Land Registry. Any solicitor or licensed conveyancer qualified in England and Wales can act. A purchase quote is explained in [conveyancing quotes in England and Wales](/blog/conveyancing-quote-explained-england-wales/).
- **Scotland.** The deed is a disposition, and a mortgage is a standard security. Your solicitor must be qualified in Scots law. [Buying a house in Scotland](/blog/buying-a-house-in-scotland-conveyancing/) explains missives and registration, which is a different process from a transfer of a share of a home you already own. LBTT, not SDLT, is the tax to check.
- **Northern Ireland.** Land Registry NI charges on the value of the share being transferred, under the Land Registry (Fees) Order (Northern Ireland) 2014. Where a registered owner transfers to themselves and another person, the fee is on the share passing to the other person. The electronic scale runs from £80 to £445. The full table is in [conveyancing costs in Northern Ireland](/blog/conveyancing-costs-northern-ireland/). Your solicitor must be qualified in Northern Ireland.

Before you instruct, ask whether the fee is fixed and includes the lender's consent, which Land Registry scale was used, and whether anyone has checked the tax on the mortgage share as well as any cash. If the firm can act for only one of you, the quote is not the whole cost. Lender "free legals" on a remortgage often exclude the change of owners.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a solicitor charge for a transfer of equity?

There is no official fee. You pay the legal fee, VAT at 20%, the land-registry fee, and any lender charge for adding or removing a borrower. Compare those totals. The tax, if any, is the same whichever firm you use.

### Do I pay stamp duty if no money changes hands?

You can. In England and Northern Ireland, taking on a share of the mortgage counts as chargeable consideration even when no cash is paid. If that share is over the Stamp Duty Land Tax threshold, tax is due. A gift with no mortgage and no cash is not normally taxed.

### Do I pay stamp duty when we transfer the house on divorce?

Not in England and Northern Ireland, if the transfer is to your spouse or civil partner under an agreement or court order because you are divorcing, dissolving a civil partnership, annulling the marriage or separating. You do not need to tell HMRC about that transfer. Wales and Scotland have their own taxes, so the conveyancer should check LTT or LBTT relief rather than assume the same rule.

### Can a transfer of equity be done with a remortgage?

Yes, and it often is, because the lender has to agree to the new borrowers. It is extra work. A lender's free legal package usually covers the remortgage only, and charges for the change of owners on top.

### Does the person coming off the title have to agree?

Yes. A transfer needs the current owners' signatures, and the lender's consent if a mortgage is being changed. One owner cannot remove the other, or take them off the mortgage, without that agreement or a court order.

### Is the process the same in Scotland?

No. A Scottish solicitor prepares a disposition, and any mortgage is a standard security registered with Registers of Scotland. The tax to check is LBTT, not Stamp Duty Land Tax. The solicitor must be qualified in Scots law.
